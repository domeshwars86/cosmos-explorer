COSMOS Labs

Interactive Astronomy Experiments

Seven hands-on labs that let you experiment with the physics behind gravity, orbits, eclipses, moon phases, seasons, light-speed delay, and habitability — instead of just reading about them.

Gravity Lab

Mass never changes — but weight and jump height depend entirely on local gravity. Enter your weight and a jump velocity to see how differently you'd move across the Solar System.

A jump velocity of ~2.5 m/s is a typical modest human jump on Earth.

Jump height is estimated with h = v² / (2g) — a simplified physics model that ignores air resistance and body mechanics.

Earth Seasons Lab

Earth's 23.4° axial tilt — not its distance from the Sun — is what causes the seasons. Move Earth around its orbit to see why.

Notice the two hemispheres always have opposite seasons — because it's the tilt toward or away from the Sun that matters, not the (nearly constant) distance.

Signal & Light-Speed Lab

Light — and every radio signal — travels at roughly 299,792 km/s. Pick a destination to see the real one-way delay, then send a test message to feel what that delay means for communication.

No message is actually transmitted anywhere — this simulates the concept of communication delay. The delay numbers shown are real physics; the on-screen animation is time-compressed for demonstration (waiting 4.24 real years for Proxima Centauri wouldn't make for a great lab).
