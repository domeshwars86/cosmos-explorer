Cosmology Concepts

Four ideas at the frontier of astrophysics — each genuinely still being studied. This page is explicit about what's observed versus what remains unknown.

Dark Matter

INFERRED

Galaxies rotate faster at their edges than visible matter alone can explain — as if surrounded by a much larger halo of unseen mass. Dark matter does not emit, absorb, or reflect light; it is never shown here as glowing material, only as an inferred gravitational structure.

Illustrative chart shape, not plotted data from a specific galaxy.

Dark Energy

OBSERVED EFFECT

Distant supernova observations in the late 1990s revealed the universe's expansion is accelerating, not slowing as gravity alone would predict. Whatever is driving that acceleration is called "dark energy" — its effect is observed; its underlying physical nature is not yet understood.

The Expanding Universe

Distant galaxies are moving apart because the space between them is expanding — not because they're flying through pre-existing empty space, like the classic analogy of dots on an inflating balloon's surface. The farther apart two points are, the faster they recede from each other, a relationship known as the Hubble-Lemaître law. This stretches light to longer (redder) wavelengths — redshift — which is how the expansion itself is measured.

The "observable universe" is the portion whose light has had time to reach us since the Big Bang — the full universe may be far larger, or even infinite; this is genuinely unknown.

Gravitational Lensing

Massive objects bend the fabric of spacetime, curving the path of light that passes near them — predicted by general relativity and confirmed by observation. A massive foreground galaxy can distort, magnify, or even multiply the image of a background galaxy into arcs or a complete "Einstein ring" when the alignment is precise.
