Beyond the Planets

Small Bodies of the Solar System

Asteroids, comets, and vast reservoirs of icy debris fill the spaces between and beyond the planets — mostly far emptier than diagrams like the one below can show.

Rocky Fragments

Asteroids

Rocky, metallic leftovers from the Solar System's formation, ranging from dust-sized fragments to bodies hundreds of kilometers across.

Asteroid Belt

Most asteroids orbit between Mars and Jupiter — though even there, the average distance between objects is roughly a million kilometers.

Near-Earth Objects

Asteroids whose orbits bring them close to Earth's — tracked closely by planetary defense programs.

Trojan Asteroids

Asteroids sharing a planet's orbit, clustered at gravitationally stable points 60° ahead or behind it — Jupiter has thousands.

Potentially Hazardous

Near-Earth asteroids large enough and close enough in orbit to warrant tracking — a classification, not a prediction of impact.

Nucleus

A "dirty snowball" of ice, dust, and rock, often just a few kilometers across.

Coma

A glowing cloud of gas and dust released as solar heat vaporizes surface ice.

Ion Tail

Charged particles pushed straight back by the solar wind — always points away from the Sun.

Dust Tail

Curved, sunlight-reflecting dust left along the comet's orbital path.

Kuiper Belt

OBSERVED

A disc-shaped region beyond Neptune, home to Pluto and countless icy bodies left over from the Solar System's formation. Many short-period comets originate here.

Oort Cloud

CONCEPTUAL MODEL

A theorized spherical shell of icy bodies extending roughly a light-year from the Sun — the source of most long-period comets. It has never been directly observed as a complete structure; its existence is inferred from the orbits of the comets it's believed to send inward.
